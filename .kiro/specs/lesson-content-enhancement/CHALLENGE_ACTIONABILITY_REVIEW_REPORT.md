## Actionability Criteria (from design.md)

According to the design document, actionable challenges must include:

1. **Immediate trigger** - Starts with "Right now", "Today", "Next time", etc.
2. **Specific, concrete action with time constraint** - Clear duration or deadline
3. **Clear success metric** - User knows when they've completed it
4. **Reflection prompt** - Includes "Notice what happens", "Track", "Write down", etc.
5. **Completable in <10 minutes** - Quick, achievable actions
6. **Word count: 50-75 words** - Concise but complete

## Recommendations

### Priority 1: Add Time Constraints (175 challenges)

**Action:** Add specific, measurable time constraints to every challenge.

**Examples:**
- "Right now, spend 5 minutes..."
- "Today, before 6pm..."
- "In your next conversation (within the next 3 hours)..."
- "Set a timer for 10 minutes and..."
- "Before you go to bed tonight..."

### Priority 2: Add Reflection Prompts (82 challenges)

**Action:** End challenges with explicit reflection prompts.

**Examples:**
- "Notice what happens when..."
- "Track how you feel afterward."
- "Write down what changed."
- "Record the result."
- "Pay attention to..."
- "Observe whether..."

### Priority 3: Add Immediate Triggers (62 challenges)

**Action:** Start challenges with action-oriented trigger words.

**Examples:**
- "Right now..."
- "Today..."
- "Next time..."
- "In your next..."
- "Before..."
- "When you..."

### Priority 4: Expand Word Count (166 challenges)

**Action:** Expand challenges from 23-39 words to the target 50-75 words while maintaining actionability.

**Strategy:**
- Add more specific instructions about HOW to complete the action
- Include what to do with the results
- Add context about why this specific action matters
- Include the reflection prompt with specific details

**Example Expansion:**

**Before (26 words):**
"Read one paragraph of anything out loud, deliberately slower than feels natural. Then read it again at normal pace. Notice the difference in how it lands."

**After (68 words):**
"Right now, grab any book or article nearby. Pick one paragraph. Set a timer for 2 minutes. Read it out loud at half your normal speed — so slow it feels almost uncomfortable. Then read the same paragraph again at your natural pace. Record yourself if you can. Notice: which version was easier to understand? Which felt more authoritative? That's the power of deliberate pacing. Try the slower pace in your next presentation."

## Pattern Analysis

### What Strong Challenges Have in Common

The challenges that come closest to meeting criteria share these characteristics:

1. **Start with action** - No preamble, straight to what to do
2. **Include "today"** - Creates immediate urgency
3. **Specify "one"** - Reduces overwhelm (one conversation, one thought, one action)
4. **End with reflection** - "Notice...", "Track...", "Write down..."
5. **Conversational tone** - Sound like a friend giving advice, not instructions

### Common Weaknesses

1. **Vague timing** - "Next time" without "today" or a deadline
2. **Missing duration** - No sense of how long the task takes
3. **Missing reflection** - Task ends without prompting awareness
4. **Too brief** - Not enough detail to feel confident doing it

---

## Suggested Fix Template

Use this template to restructure weak challenges:

```
[TRIGGER: Right now/Today/Next time] [TIME CONSTRAINT: spend X minutes/before X/in your next X], 
[SPECIFIC ACTION: detailed, concrete steps]. 
[SUCCESS METRIC: how you know you did it]. 
[REFLECTION PROMPT: Notice/Track/Write down] [what to pay attention to].
```

**Example Application:**

**Original (weak):**
"Think of something you want to persuade someone of. Write down their three biggest objections. Address each one honestly. That's your persuasion strategy."

**Improved:**
"Right now, spend 5 minutes on this: think of one thing you want to persuade someone of this week. Write down their three biggest objections. For each objection, write one honest response that addresses their concern without dismissing it. Read your responses out loud. Notice: do they sound defensive or do they sound curious? That curiosity is what makes persuasion work. Use these responses in your next conversation."
