# Growthovo Lesson Content Style Guide

## Overview
This guide documents the voice, tone, structure, and quality standards for all Growthovo lesson content. Use this as a reference when creating new lessons or enhancing existing content.

---

## Voice & Tone Principles

### Core Voice Attributes
- **Direct**: Say what you mean. No fluff, no hedging.
- **Conversational**: Write like you're talking to a friend over coffee.
- **Empowering**: Build confidence, never condescend.
- **Real**: Use concrete examples, not hypotheticals.
- **You-focused**: Speak directly to the reader with "you" language.

### What We Sound Like
✅ **Good**: "Your body isn't designed for sitting — it's designed for movement."  
❌ **Bad**: "Research indicates that the human body has evolved for ambulatory activities rather than sedentary postures."

✅ **Good**: "Five minutes is infinitely more than zero."  
❌ **Bad**: "Even brief exercise sessions can provide meaningful health benefits."

### Tone Guidelines
- **Short sentences** over long ones
- **Active voice** over passive
- **Concrete words** over abstract
- **Second person** ("you") over third person ("one," "people")
- **Present tense** when possible

### What to Avoid
❌ Corporate speak: "leverage," "synergy," "optimize," "utilize"  
❌ Academic jargon without explanation  
❌ Hedge words: "perhaps," "maybe," "it might be"  
❌ Filler: "basically," "actually," "essentially"  
❌ Preachy tone: "you should," "you must," "you need to"

## Lesson Structure

Every lesson contains exactly 5 cards in this order:

### 1. Concept Card (75-100 words)
**Purpose**: Explain the core idea and why it matters

**Structure**:
1. **Hook** (1 sentence): Surprising, counterintuitive, or attention-grabbing opening
2. **Mechanism** (2-3 sentences): How it works or why it's true
3. **Specifics** (2-3 sentences): Concrete details or examples
4. **Metaphor/Comparison** (optional): Make it memorable
5. **Takeaway** (1 sentence): What this means for the reader

**Template**:
```
[Surprising/counterintuitive opening]. [Why this is true]. [Core mechanism or insight]. [What this means for the user]. [The mindset shift required].
```

**Example** (Fitness - Movement):
```
Your body isn't designed for sitting — it's designed for movement. Humans evolved walking 15-20 kilometers per day hunting and gathering. Your muscles, joints, and cardiovascular system all deteriorate without regular movement, not because you're aging, but because you're not using them. Sitting for 8 hours a day is like keeping a sports car in a garage and wondering why the engine seizes. Movement isn't optional exercise — it's basic maintenance for the machine you live in.
```

### 2. Example Card (75-100 words)
**Purpose**: Show real transformation through a specific character

**Structure**:
1. **Character** (name, age, situation)
2. **Struggle**: What wasn't working before
3. **Action**: Specific thing they did differently
4. **Outcome**: Measurable change in timeframe
5. **Insight**: Their one-sentence reflection or what others noticed

**Template**:
```
[Name, age, situation]. [What wasn't working]. [What they tried instead - specific]. [Outcome in X timeframe]. [Their reflection or what others noticed].
```

**Example** (Communication - Hard Conversations):
```
Lena, 22, avoided telling her roommate the apartment was too loud. She hinted, left passive-aggressive notes, nothing changed. Finally, she said: "I need the place quiet after 10pm for work. Can we figure that out?" Her roommate said, "I didn't know — yeah, let's do it." Problem solved in 30 seconds. Lena said: "I wasted 3 months being indirect when being direct took half a minute." Two years later, they're still roommates. Directness didn't ruin the relationship. It saved it.
```

### 3. Mistake Card (75-100 words)
**Purpose**: Prevent common errors by explaining WHY they fail

**Structure**:
1. **The Mistake**: What people commonly do wrong
2. **Why It Fails**: The mechanism of failure (not just "it doesn't work")
3. **The Trap**: Why this mistake is so tempting or common
4. **The Alternative**: Brief pointer to what works instead

**Template**:
```
[Common mistake described]. [Why people do this - seems logical]. [Why it actually fails - mechanism]. [Brief pointer to correct approach].
```

**Example** (Mind - Anxiety):
```
Avoiding anxiety-triggering situations hoping the anxiety will go away. It won't. Avoidance teaches your brain that the situation is dangerous, which strengthens the fear response. Your anxiety gets worse, not better. The trap: avoidance feels like relief in the moment. The catch: it compounds the problem long-term. The fix: exposure — facing the situation in small, manageable doses — is the only thing that actually reduces anxiety over time.
```

### 4. Science Card (75-100 words)
**Purpose**: Validate the concept with research and explain the mechanism

**Structure**:
1. **Research Citation**: Institution or researcher name
2. **Finding**: Specific, measurable outcome (include numbers when possible)
3. **Mechanism**: How/why it works at biological/psychological level
4. **Translation**: What this means in plain language

**Template**:
```
[Institution/researcher] found that [specific finding with numbers if possible]. [Mechanism - the how/why]. [Translation to practical meaning].
```

**Example** (Money - Compound Interest):
```
Research from Morningstar shows that $10,000 invested at age 25 at 7% annual returns grows to $149,000 by age 65. The same $10,000 invested at age 35 grows to only $76,000 — half as much for a 10-year delay. The mechanism: compound interest means your returns generate their own returns. Every year you wait costs exponentially more than the year before. Time in the market beats timing the market because decades of compounding can't be replicated by years of higher returns.
```

### 5. Challenge Card (50-75 words)
**Purpose**: Give immediate, actionable next step

**Structure**:
1. **Trigger**: "Right now," "Today," "Next time," "Tomorrow"
2. **Action**: One specific, concrete task with time constraint
3. **Success Metric** (optional): How they know they did it right
4. **Reflection**: "Notice what happens" or similar observation prompt

**Template**:
```
[Immediate trigger]. [Specific action with time constraint]. [Optional: how to know you did it right]. Notice [what to pay attention to / what changes].
```

**Example** (Career - Personal Branding):
```
Open LinkedIn right now. Write one post about something you learned this week related to your field. No more than 200 words. Don't overthink it — just publish. Notice how it feels to put your thoughts public. That discomfort is the gap between where you are and where you want to be. Cross it.
```

## Word Count Standards

**Strict Limits**:
- Concept: 75-100 words
- Example: 75-100 words  
- Mistake: 75-100 words
- Science: 75-100 words
- Challenge: 50-75 words
- Title: <50 characters

**Why These Limits Matter**:
- Mobile reading experience
- Attention span optimization
- Forces clarity and concision
- Consistent user experience

**How to Check**:
Use any word counter tool. If over, cut ruthlessly:
- Remove filler words
- Combine sentences
- Choose shorter words
- Cut redundant phrases

## Content Quality Checklist

Before finalizing any lesson, verify:

### Concept Card
- [ ] Opens with surprising/counterintuitive hook
- [ ] Explains mechanism clearly
- [ ] Under 100 words
- [ ] No jargon without explanation

### Example Card
- [ ] Names a real-feeling character (name + age)
- [ ] Shows clear before/after transformation
- [ ] Includes specific timeframe (weeks/months)
- [ ] Ends with character insight or quote
- [ ] Feels authentic, not invented

### Mistake Card
- [ ] Explains WHY the mistake fails (mechanism)
- [ ] Compassionate tone ("many people" not "stupid people")
- [ ] Points toward correct approach
- [ ] Prevents wasted time/effort

### Science Card
- [ ] Cites real institution or established framework
- [ ] Includes specific numbers when possible
- [ ] Explains mechanism, not just correlation
- [ ] Translates to plain language

### Challenge Card
- [ ] Starts with immediate trigger word
- [ ] Specific and completable in <10 minutes
- [ ] Clear success criteria
- [ ] Includes reflection prompt
- [ ] No vague instructions ("try to," "work on")

## Character Name Guidelines

**Diversity Standards**:
Use names representing various cultural/geographic origins:
- European: Liam, Ella, Sophie, James
- Mediterranean/Latin: Sofia, Marcus, Elena, Luca
- Arabic/Middle Eastern: Aisha, Omar, Zara
- South Asian: Priya, Dev, Arjun
- Multi-cultural: Kai, Noah, Maya

**Age Range**: 18-25 (target demographic)

**Gender Balance**: Roughly equal representation across lessons

**Avoid**:
- Stereotypical pairings (names + situations that reinforce biases)
- Same names repeated across lessons
- Names that are difficult to pronounce/spell

## Inclusive Language Standards

### Body Image & Fitness
✅ **Use**: "stronger," "healthier," "more capable," "feel better," "perform better"  
❌ **Avoid**: "lose weight," "get skinny," "beach body," "six-pack," "toned"

✅ **Use**: "eating for performance," "fueling your body," "sustainable eating"  
❌ **Avoid**: "cheat meal," "guilty pleasure," "bad food," "clean eating"

### Mental Health
✅ **Use**: "anxiety response," "thought patterns," "emotional regulation"  
❌ **Avoid**: "crazy," "insane," "mental," "psycho"

✅ **Use**: "many people experience," "common response," "brain's alarm system"  
❌ **Avoid**: "you're broken," "what's wrong with you," "normal vs abnormal"

### Ability & Accessibility
✅ **Use**: "start where you are," "any level," "scale to your needs"  
❌ **Avoid**: "just," "simply," "easy," "anyone can"

✅ **Use**: "here's one approach," "this worked for X," "consider trying"  
❌ **Avoid**: "you should," "you must," "the only way"

### Gender & Relationships
✅ **Use**: "partner," context-neutral language  
❌ **Avoid**: Heteronormative assumptions (his girlfriend/her boyfriend)

### Socioeconomic Sensitivity
✅ **Use**: "free options," "no equipment needed," "accessible approaches"  
❌ **Avoid**: Assuming gym access, equipment, disposable income

## Science Standards

### Citation Requirements
**Always include**:
- Institution name OR researcher/study name
- Specific finding with numbers
- Mechanism (how/why it works)

**Good**: "Stanford research shows that 20 minutes of moderate exercise triggers BDNF release..."  
**Bad**: "Studies show exercise is good for you."

### Verification Standards
- Cite real research (no invented studies)
- Use established frameworks when specific studies unavailable
- Explain mechanisms, not just correlations
- Accessible language (define technical terms)

### Common Frameworks to Use
- **Psychology**: CBT principles, habit formation (Duhigg), neuroplasticity
- **Fitness**: Progressive overload, SAID principle, supercompensation
- **Money**: Compound interest, opportunity cost, time value of money
- **Communication**: Active listening, nonverbal communication research

## Quality Comparison Examples

### WEAK vs STRONG Concept
❌ **Weak**: "Exercise is important for health and can help you feel better. Many studies show that regular physical activity has benefits."

✅ **Strong**: "Your body isn't designed for sitting — it's designed for movement. Humans evolved walking 15-20 kilometers per day. Sitting for 8 hours a day is like keeping a sports car in a garage and wondering why the engine seizes."

**Why Strong Wins**: Specific hook, vivid metaphor, explains mechanism, no filler.

### WEAK vs STRONG Example
❌ **Weak**: "John started exercising and felt better. He was happy with his progress."

✅ **Strong**: "Marcus, 21, couldn't do a single push-up in January. He started with wall push-ups, then knee push-ups, then negatives. By March he did 10 full push-ups. His friends asked what changed. He said: 'I just showed up every day.'"

**Why Strong Wins**: Specific name/age, clear progression, timeframe, concrete outcome, character voice.

### WEAK vs STRONG Science
❌ **Weak**: "Research shows that meditation helps reduce stress."

✅ **Strong**: "Harvard research found that 8 weeks of daily meditation reduced amygdala activity (the brain's fear center) by 22% on MRI scans. The mechanism: repeated practice teaches your brain that thoughts are just thoughts, not threats. Your stress response literally shrinks."

**Why Strong Wins**: Specific institution, measurable finding, mechanism explained, practical translation.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Pitfall 1: Vague Language
❌ "Try to work on improving your communication skills"  
✅ "Next conversation, pause 2 seconds before responding"

### Pitfall 2: Hypothetical Examples
❌ "Imagine if you started saving $100 per month..."  
✅ "Elena, 22, saved €50 per month starting in January..."

### Pitfall 3: Unclear Science
❌ "Studies show this works"  
✅ "University of Queensland research shows..."

### Pitfall 4: Preachy Tone
❌ "You need to start exercising regularly if you want to be healthy"  
✅ "Movement isn't optional exercise — it's basic maintenance"

### Pitfall 5: Weak Challenges
❌ "Try to be more mindful this week"  
✅ "Set a timer for 5 minutes right now. Sit still and count your breaths."

## Revision Checklist

When reviewing any lesson, ask:

**Voice & Tone**:
- [ ] Would I say this to a friend?
- [ ] Is every word necessary?
- [ ] Does it sound like a real person wrote this?

**Structure**:
- [ ] Does the concept hook immediately?
- [ ] Does the example feel real and specific?
- [ ] Does the mistake explain WHY it fails?
- [ ] Does the science cite real research?
- [ ] Is the challenge immediately actionable?

**Inclusivity**:
- [ ] Diverse character names?
- [ ] No assumptions about gender/sexuality/ability/background?
- [ ] Accessible language and examples?
- [ ] Body-positive framing (fitness content)?

**Quality**:
- [ ] All word counts within limits?
- [ ] No placeholder text or TODOs?
- [ ] Consistent formatting?
- [ ] No typos or grammar errors?

## Future Lesson Creation Workflow

1. **Research** (10 min): Find real studies or frameworks
2. **Draft Concept** (10 min): Hook + mechanism + takeaway
3. **Create Example** (10 min): Real-feeling character with transformation
4. **Write Mistake** (5 min): Common error + why it fails
5. **Document Science** (5 min): Institution + finding + mechanism
6. **Design Challenge** (5 min): Specific action + reflection
7. **Edit** (10 min): Cut to word limits, verify checklist
8. **Read Aloud** (5 min): Does it sound conversational?

**Total Time per Lesson**: ~60 minutes for quality content
