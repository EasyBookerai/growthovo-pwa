## 3. Body Image & Fitness Content Review

### Critical Assessment: No Toxic Diet Culture Language

**✅ PASSED - EXCELLENT:** Fitness content explicitly REJECTS harmful body image messaging

**Specific Findings:**

**Harmful language ABSENT:**
- ❌ "Lose weight" - Not used as a goal
- ❌ "Get skinny" - Not present
- ❌ "Burn fat" - Not present
- ❌ "Beach body" - Not present
- ❌ "Summer body" - Not present (except in explicit rejection)
- ❌ "Six pack" - Not present
- ❌ "Toned" - Not present
- ❌ "Slim down" - Not present
- ❌ "Cheat meal/cheat day" - Not present
- ❌ "Guilty eating" - Not present
- ❌ "Bad food/good food" - Not present

**✅ STRENGTH:** Single mention of "shredded" appears in REJECTION context:
> "The goal isn't to be shredded by summer. It's to be stronger, healthier, and more capable at 35 than you were at 25."

This explicitly reframes away from appearance goals toward functional, health-based goals.

**✅ STRENGTH:** Fitness messaging consistently emphasizes:
- **Performance over appearance:** "feeling stronger" not "looking better"
- **Health metrics:** heart rate, mobility, energy, sleep quality
- **Functional goals:** "climb stairs pain-free," "play with grandkids," "move without pain at 65"
- **Accessibility:** "your body at any size," "start where you are"
- **Sustainability:** "train for decades" not "transform in 90 days"

**✅ STRENGTH:** Nutrition content focuses on:
- Macros for performance (not weight loss)
- Energy and function (not appearance)
- Sustainable eating (not restriction or dieting)
- "Eating for Performance vs Eating for Weight" as a comparison lesson
- Body Image Trap lesson explicitly addresses toxic messaging

**Example of Excellent Body-Positive Framing (Lesson: Walking as Exercise):**
> "Walking doesn't feel like exercise because it's not hard. But hard isn't the only metric that matters... Stop dismissing it as 'not enough' and start using it consistently."

This validates low-intensity movement for all body types and fitness levels.

## 4. Situational Diversity & Life Contexts

### Life Stage Representation

**✅ STRENGTH:** Examples span diverse life situations:
- **Students:** University seminars, class presentations, finals week
- **Early career:** Job interviews, first jobs, workplace meetings, startups
- **Varied family structures:** Living with roommates, alone, gaming after work

### Accessibility of Situations

**✅ STRENGTH:** Examples avoid assumptions about privilege:
- Fitness: "no gym, no equipment needed"
- Money: Examples start with €50/month savings, not large investments
- Career: Examples include freelancers, students, entry-level employees
- Relationships: Examples include platonic friendships, roommates, colleagues

**Minor Gap:** 
- Most examples feature individuals who appear to have stable housing and internet access
- Recommendation: Consider 1-2 examples featuring individuals navigating instability (job loss, housing insecurity) to validate those experiences

## 5. Language Assumptions & Ableist Patterns

### "Just" and "Simply" Language Check

**✅ MOSTLY PASSED:** Language avoids minimizing difficulty

**Pattern Found:** Minimal use of "just" that minimizes:
- "Just walking" - used neutrally to describe simplicity, not dismissively
- "Just move" - used to remove barriers, not minimize effort

**Context matters:** Most uses of "just" and "simply" are explanatory ("it's just X, not Y") rather than dismissive ("just do X, it's easy").

### "Normal" Language Check

**✅ PASSED:** No "normal people" language detected
- Content avoids framing behaviors as "normal vs abnormal"
- Uses "most people" or "many people" instead
- Validates diverse experiences without pathologizing
